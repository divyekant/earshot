Westmere votes to keep its night ferry for three more years

By Odile Farrant · 14 March · 4 minute read

The town council of Westmere voted on Tuesday evening to keep the late ferry to Corran Island until the end of the next contract period. Nine councillors voted for the plan and four voted against it. The ferry leaves the north pier at 23:40 each night and comes back at 00:25.

The council had to decide before the end of March, because the current operator, Brisk Water Lines, gave notice that it would stop the night crossing without a new subsidy. The new contract costs the town 212,000 pounds a year, which is 38,000 pounds more than the old one.

Why the crossing matters

About 140 people use the night ferry on a normal weekday. Most of them work late shifts at the hospital, in the harbour kitchens or at the fish market. A survey by the council in January found that 61 of the regular passengers have no other way to get home after 22:00.

Nurse Teodor Vance, who lives on Corran and works nights on the children's ward, spoke at the meeting. He said that without the ferry he would have to rent a second room in town for four nights each week. Several other speakers told similar stories.

The cost and the objections

Councillor Maren Holt, who voted against the plan, said the subsidy works out at more than five pounds for each trip. She asked the council to test a smaller boat in the winter months, when fewer people travel. The ferry operator said that a smaller boat could not carry the night delivery van for the island shop.

Two councillors asked for a review after eighteen months. The final plan includes that review. If the number of passengers falls below 90 on an average weekday, the council can end the contract with six months of notice.

What happens next

  • The council signs the new contract with Brisk Water Lines before 31 March.
  • Ticket prices stay at 3.20 pounds for adults and 1.60 pounds for children.
  • A new shelter with a light and a bench goes up at the north pier in the summer.

The harbour master said the timetable for the summer season will be published in May, with no change to the night crossing.
