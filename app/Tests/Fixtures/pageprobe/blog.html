A year of tomatoes on a small balcony
Posted by Ines Calloway on 2 October
Last spring I had a balcony of two square metres, a north-west view and no garden experience. I wanted to know if a person in a flat could grow enough tomatoes to stop buying them from July to October. The short answer is yes, but only with three plants, big pots and a lot of water.
The plants I chose
I grew two cherry plants of a variety called Sunpearl and one larger plant called Red Lantern. The cherry plants gave fruit five weeks before the large one. In total I picked 6.4 kilograms of tomatoes, and 4.9 kilograms of that came from the two cherry plants.
Pots, soil and water
Each plant had a pot of 30 litres. Smaller pots dried out in two hours on hot days. I mixed garden compost with one part of coarse sand so the water could drain. In July and August the plants needed water twice a day, at about seven in the morning and again after eight in the evening.
The one change that helped most was a saucer under each pot. The saucer held the extra water for the afternoon. Before I used saucers, the leaves hung down by three o'clock on most sunny days.
What went wrong
In late August the lower leaves of the large plant got brown spots. I removed every leaf below the first fruit and kept the leaves dry when I watered. The spots stopped, but the plant gave few new flowers after that. Next year I will put the large plant at the end of the balcony with the most wind.
Pigeons were the other problem. They took green cherry tomatoes and dropped them on the floor. A light net over the top of the plants solved this in one day.
Would I do it again?
Yes. The cost was 74 pounds for pots, soil, seeds, the net and the saucers, and most of that I can use again. For a balcony like mine I would grow only cherry plants, start the seeds indoors in the middle of March, and plant them out after the middle of May.
