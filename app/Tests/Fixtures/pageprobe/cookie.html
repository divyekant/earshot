Four quiet walks around Lake Orrin

Lake Orrin is busy on summer weekends near the boat house, but most of its shore is quiet all year. These four walks start from small car parks and avoid the main road. Each walk is a loop, so you come back to your car.

1. The reed bed loop, 3 kilometres

Start at the car park by the old pump house on Mill Lane. The path follows a wooden walkway through the reed beds on the west shore. In April and May you can hear reed warblers. The walkway is flat and suits a pushchair, but it can flood after heavy rain.

2. Heron Point and the beech wood, 5 kilometres

From the lay-by on Fenwick Road, walk north along the shore to Heron Point, where a bench looks over the whole lake. The path then climbs into an old beech wood. The climb is short but steep, and the roots make it slippery in autumn.

3. The east meadows, 4 kilometres

This walk crosses three meadows that the parish cuts only once a year, in late July. In June the meadows are full of orchids. Keep dogs on a lead from March to July, because skylarks nest on the ground here.

4. The full shore, 11 kilometres

For a long day, link the first three walks with the path along the south shore. Allow four hours. The only cafe on the route is at the boat house, which closes at five in the afternoon from October to March.

Before you go, check the board at each car park. The ranger posts closures there when the forestry team works in the beech wood.
