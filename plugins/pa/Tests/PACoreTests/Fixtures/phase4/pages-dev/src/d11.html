How to replace a bathroom tap washer
Difficulty: easy · Time: 30 minutes · Updated 15 August
A tap that drips after you close it usually has a worn washer. A new washer costs less than a pound, and you can fit it with two spanners and a screwdriver. This guide is for older taps with separate hot and cold heads, not for mixer taps with a ceramic cartridge.
Step 1: Turn off the water. Close the isolation valve under the basin with a flat screwdriver, or close the stop tap of the house. Open the tap to let the last water out.
Step 2: Remove the head. Prise off the cover on top of the tap, undo the screw under it, and lift off the head. Put the plug in the basin so small parts cannot fall into the drain.
Step 3: Undo the headgear nut. Hold the tap body with one spanner and turn the large nut anticlockwise with the other. Do not let the whole tap turn, because this can crack the basin.
Step 4: Change the washer. The washer sits at the bottom of the headgear, held by a small nut or pressed on. Take the old washer to the shop to match the size; most taps use 12 mm or 19 mm washers.
Step 5: Put it back together. Fit the headgear, the head and the cover in the reverse order, then turn the water on slowly and check for drips.
