Episode 41: Why do old houses get damp?

Released 3 March · 52 minutes

▶ Play · 00:00 / 52:10 · Download · Speed 1x

Building surveyor Ngozi Hartley explains why houses built before 1919 often get damp, and why the common fixes can make the problem worse. Solid brick walls were made to take in rain and dry out again. Cement render, plastic paint and a chemical damp-proof course can stop the drying, so the water stays in the wall.

In this episode

  • How to tell rising damp from condensation and from a leaking gutter
  • Why a hygrometer of £15 is the first tool to buy
  • Lime plaster and lime paint, and when they are worth the cost
  • The one-week test: keep a window open a little and the heating at 18 degrees, and watch the readings

Ngozi's main advice: fix the outside first. Clear the gutters, check that the ground outside is at least 15 cm below the inside floor, and only then think about the walls.
