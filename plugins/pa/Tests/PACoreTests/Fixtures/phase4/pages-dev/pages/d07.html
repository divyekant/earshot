Ruskmoor Lighthouse

Ruskmoor Lighthouse
LocationRuskmoor Head
Built1861
Height27 m
Range18 nautical miles
Automated1989

Ruskmoor Lighthouse is a stone lighthouse on the cliffs of Ruskmoor Head. It was built in 1861 after two ships were lost on the Ruskmoor Ledges in the same winter. The tower is 27 metres tall, and its white light can be seen 18 nautical miles out to sea.

History

The engineer Josiah Pellow designed the tower with walls that are 2 metres thick at the base. Three keepers and their families lived in the cottages next to it until 1989, when the light was automated. The last principal keeper, Albert Treen, served at Ruskmoor for 22 years.

In 1944 the lamp was hit by a storm wave that broke two panes of the lantern. The keepers kept a smaller lamp burning for nine days until new glass arrived by boat.

The lighthouse today

The cottages are now a small museum, open from April to October. Visitors can climb the 117 steps of the tower on guided tours, which run twice a day in the summer.[1]
