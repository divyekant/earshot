Mending a wool jumper with a darning mushroom
Written by Harriet Moss · 11 January
My favourite grey jumper got a hole in the left elbow this winter. It is about the size of a coin. Instead of a patch, I darned it, and the repair took about forty minutes. Here is the method I use, with the mistakes I made the first few times.
You need a darning mushroom or a smooth round object such as a small orange, a blunt needle with a large eye, and wool of about the same thickness as the jumper. The colour does not have to match. A contrast colour can look good on an elbow.
First, put the mushroom inside the sleeve under the hole. Do not stretch the knitting over it. Then sew a line of small running stitches in a circle around the hole, about one centimetre away from its edge. This circle holds the weak stitches near the hole.
Next, make the warp. Sew straight lines from one side of the circle to the other, close together, across the hole. Leave a small loop at each turn so the darn can stretch with the jumper. Then weave the weft: go over and under the warp lines at a right angle, and push each row tight against the last one with the needle.
The mistake I made most often was pulling the wool too tight. A tight darn puckers, and the knitting around it tears again within weeks. The darn should lie flat and feel a little loose when the mushroom is out.
