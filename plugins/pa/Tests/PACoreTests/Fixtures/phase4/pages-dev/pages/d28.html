Lesson 3: Reading a contour map

A contour line joins points of the same height above sea level. On most walking maps of 1:25,000, the lines are 5 metres apart in height on low ground and 10 metres apart in mountains. The map key tells you which one your map uses.

Steep or gentle?

When contour lines are close together, the ground is steep. When they are far apart, the ground is gentle. Where lines almost touch, expect a cliff or a very steep bank.

Up or down?

The height numbers on the lines face uphill: the top of the number points up the slope. Rivers and streams always run downhill, so a stream on the map tells you which way the ground falls.

Valleys and spurs

Contour lines make a V or a U shape where they cross a valley or a spur. In a valley, the point of the V faces uphill. On a spur, the point faces downhill.

Check yourself

Question 1 of 3: The lines are close together. Is the slope steep or gentle?
