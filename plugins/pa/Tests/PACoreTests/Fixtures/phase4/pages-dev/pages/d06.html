Grandmother Beatriz's lentil soup with lemon

Posted by Carmen Duarte on 4 November · Serves 4

When I was nine, my family spent a whole winter in my grandmother's house in the hills. The house had one stove, and the stove always had a pot on it. Most days that pot was this soup. She stirred it with a wooden spoon that had a burnt end, and she never measured anything. I asked her many times how much lemon she put in, and she always said "enough".

Years later I tried to make it from memory, and it took me about twenty attempts to get close. My mother helped with the cumin, which I had forgotten completely. My brother says it is still not right. He may be correct, but this is the version my children ask for.

A note on lentils: the small brown ones work best here. Red lentils turn to mush, which is not bad, but it is a different soup.

Ingredients

  • 250 g small brown lentils, rinsed
  • 1 large onion, chopped
  • 2 carrots, in small cubes
  • 3 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1.5 litres water or light stock
  • Juice of 1 lemon, and salt

Method

  1. Warm the oil in a large pot and cook the onion and carrots on a low heat for 10 minutes, until soft.
  2. Add the cumin and stir for one minute.
  3. Add the lentils and the water. Bring to the boil, then cook gently for 35 to 40 minutes, until the lentils are soft.
  4. Blend about a third of the soup and stir it back in, so the soup is thick but not smooth.
  5. Add the lemon juice and salt just before you serve.
Can I freeze it?

Yes, for up to three months. Add the lemon after you heat it again.
