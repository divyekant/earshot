The best headlamps for winter running: 5 that we tested

We buy all products with our own money. If you buy through a link on this page, we can earn a commission. Read more

By Yusuf Brandt · Updated 20 October 2026

We ran with five headlamps for six weeks on unlit paths and fell tracks, in rain and at temperatures down to minus 4 °C. We measured the real run time at full power with a light meter, because the numbers on the box are almost never correct.

The short answer

The Corvid Trail 600 is the best headlamp for most runners: it is bright, it does not bounce, and it gave 3 hours 40 minutes at full power. If you want to spend less, the Lumo Dash 300 at £28 is enough for paths and towpaths. For long nights in the hills, take the Petrasol Ridge 1100 with its battery at the back of the head.

Best for most runners: Corvid Trail 600

The Corvid Trail 600 has a wide beam for the ground at your feet and a spot for the distance, and you can use the two together. The band has a silicone line on the inside and did not move on any run. You can use the one large button with thick gloves. It charges by USB-C in 2 hours. Check price at Fellfoot Shop Check price at Ridgeline

What we do not like: the battery cannot be changed, and the red rear light is an extra that costs £9.

Best cheap headlamp: Lumo Dash 300

The Lumo Dash 300 weighs 58 g, less than each other lamp in the test. The beam is narrow, so it is not good for rough ground, and it is only splash-proof (IPX4). For runs under two hours on paths it is all that you need. Check price at Fellfoot Shop

Best for the hills: Petrasol Ridge 1100

With the Petrasol Ridge 1100 we could see path markers at 120 metres. The battery pack at the back makes the weight even, and you can change it for a spare (£24) in the dark by feel. It is heavy for a short run and the price is high. It is waterproof to IPX7, and it worked after 30 minutes in a bucket of water. Check price at Ridgeline

The two that we do not recommend

The Nightjar Flex 450 became dim after less than two hours, and its band came loose when it was wet. The Okta Beam Pro is a good light with a bad button: we turned it off by accident on five of twelve runs.

How we tested

Each lamp was charged fully and run at full power at 5 °C until the light fell below half of its start value. Three runners with different head sizes wore each lamp for at least 40 kilometres.

Yusuf Brandt has tested running gear for Fellfoot since 2019. He runs about 70 km a week, most of it before sunrise.
