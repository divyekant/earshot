Harrowmere is a market town in the West Fells region of Northland. It stands at the north end of the lake of the same name, 34 kilometres west of the regional capital, Dunmarrow. At the census of 2021 the town had 11,840 inhabitants.

The town grew around a wool market that received its charter in 1284. From the 1790s to the 1950s it was known for the manufacture of pencils, which used graphite from the mines at Sable Crag. Today the largest employers are tourism and a regional hospital.

History [edit]

A settlement at the lake is first recorded in a land register of 1131 as Harwemere. King Aldric III gave the town the right to hold a weekly market in 1284. The market cross that stands in the square today is a copy of 1867; the original is in the town museum.

Graphite was found at Sable Crag in about 1560. The first pencil workshop opened in 1792, and by 1850 the town had eleven pencil factories with some 600 workers. The last factory, Fell & Corrie, closed in 1958. A fire in 1883 destroyed most of the wooden houses on the east side of the square, which were built again in stone.

Geography [edit]

Harrowmere lies in a glacial valley between Sable Crag (671 m) to the west and Long Rigg (588 m) to the east. The River Harrow flows out of the lake through the town centre. The town has an average of 1,480 mm of rain each year, which makes it one of the wettest towns in the country.

Economy [edit]

About 420,000 visitors come to the town each year, most of them between May and September. The West Fells General Hospital, which opened in 1974, employs about 900 people. A weekly market is still held on Thursdays.

Transport [edit]

The railway line to Dunmarrow closed in 1966. Its route is now a path for walkers and cyclists. Bus route X5 connects the town with Dunmarrow in 55 minutes. A passenger boat crosses the lake to Southwater four times a day in summer.
