Council approves repair of Millgate footbridge

By Tomasz Winter, local democracy reporter · 3 November 2026, 18:42

The Millgate footbridge will be repaired and opened again by the end of March, after councillors voted on Tuesday evening to release £412,000 from the highways reserve.

The bridge over the River Sorrel has been closed since February, when an inspection found corrosion in two of the steel bearings. People who walk from the Ashdown estate to the town centre now use the road bridge at Tanner Street, which adds about 15 minutes to the trip.

The vote was nine to four. Councillor Edith Marlowe, who leads the highways committee, said the repair was cheaper than a new bridge by more than a million pounds. "We looked at a replacement and the lowest estimate was £1.6 million," she said. "This repair gives us at least 25 more years."

The four councillors who voted against the plan wanted to wait for a grant decision from the regional transport fund, which is expected in January. Councillor Dev Patel said the council could have saved the full amount. "If the grant comes, we have spent our own reserve for nothing," he said.

The contractor, Brookfield Structures, will start work on 11 January. The riverside path below the bridge will be closed on weekdays between 08:00 and 16:30 while the work continues. A signed diversion will go along Weir Lane.

Residents can see the drawings at the town library until 28 November. Comments on the path closure must reach the highways office by 5 December.

What happens next

The council will publish the full timetable before Christmas. If the regional grant is approved in January, the money will go back into the reserve, a council spokesperson said.
