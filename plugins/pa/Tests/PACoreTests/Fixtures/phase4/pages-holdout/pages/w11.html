Why does my sourdough loaf have a dense, gummy layer at the bottom?

Asked 3 weeks ago · Modified 16 days ago · Viewed 2,104 times
▲
17
▼

I bake one sourdough loaf each week in a cast iron pot. The top half of the loaf is open and light. The bottom centimetre is dense and wet, almost like raw dough, though the crust below it is dark.

My recipe: 500 g strong white flour, 375 g water, 100 g starter, 10 g salt. Bulk rise 5 hours at about 21 °C, shape, then 14 hours in the fridge. I bake at 240 °C for 25 minutes with the lid on and 20 minutes with the lid off, straight from the fridge.

The starter doubles in 6 hours. What causes the gummy layer?

A dense band at the bottom with an open top is the classic sign of an underproofed loaf, not of a problem with the oven.

A starter that needs 6 hours to double is slow. With only 20 percent starter at 21 °C, 5 hours of bulk rise is not enough. The dough has not made enough gas, so the gas that is there rises to the top in the oven and the bottom collapses under the weight.

Do not go by the clock. Let the dough rise until its volume is about 75 percent larger and the surface has a dome with some bubbles. In your kitchen that can take 7 to 8 hours. A straight-sided container with a mark on it makes this easy to see.

Also feed the starter two times on the day before you bake, so that it doubles in about 4 hours.

answered by Hartmut Ojo · 18.2k reputation
This was it. 7.5 hours and the layer is gone. Thank you! – Leontien V.

The other answer is correct about the proof. One more point: a cast iron pot that is not hot enough at its base gives the same fault in a weaker form. Heat the pot for a full 45 minutes, and put a baking sheet on the shelf below it only if the base burns.
