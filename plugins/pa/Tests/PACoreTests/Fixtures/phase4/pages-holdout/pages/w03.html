Red lentil dal with burnt garlic

By Noor Halvorsen · Published 2 March 2025 · Updated 8 September 2026 · 46 comments

Jump to recipe Print

My grandmother made this dal on the days when the fishing boats did not go out. There was no fish, the shop was a long walk away, and there was always a jar of red lentils on the shelf above the stove.

She did one thing that I have not seen in any cookbook. She fried the garlic much longer than you think is correct, until the slices were dark brown at the edges and the kitchen smelled almost of smoke. Then she poured the oil and the garlic over the dal at the table.

For years I made the dal with pale garlic, because every recipe says that brown garlic is bitter. It was fine. It was never hers. When I at last burned a batch by accident, I knew at once what was missing.

Red lentils need no soaking, and they are soft in about 20 minutes. That makes this a good dinner for a weekday. It is also cheap: the full pot costs less than two pounds.

Red lentil dal with burnt garlic

Serves 4 · Preparation 10 minutes · Cooking 25 minutes

Ingredients

  • 300 g red lentils
  • 1.1 litres water
  • 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
  • 1 tin (400 g) chopped tomatoes
  • 1 and a half teaspoons salt
  • 4 tablespoons sunflower oil
  • 1 teaspoon cumin seeds
  • 6 large cloves of garlic, in thin slices
  • 2 dried red chillies
  • Juice of half a lemon

Method

  1. Wash the lentils in a sieve until the water is almost clear.
  2. Put the lentils, the water and the turmeric in a large pan. Bring to the boil and remove the foam with a spoon.
  3. Simmer for 15 minutes with the lid half on. Add the tomatoes and the salt, and simmer for 5 more minutes. Beat the dal with a whisk for a few seconds so that it becomes creamy.
  4. Heat the oil in a small frying pan on a medium heat. Add the cumin seeds. When they start to crackle, add the garlic and the chillies.
  5. Fry the garlic for 3 to 4 minutes, and move it all the time, until the edges are dark brown. Do not stop at golden.
  6. Pour the hot oil and the garlic over the dal. Add the lemon juice and serve with rice or flatbread.

Notes

The dal keeps for 3 days in the fridge and becomes thick. Add a little water when you heat it again. You can freeze it for up to 2 months, but make the garlic oil fresh.
