How to bleed a radiator: step by step

If a radiator is hot at the bottom and cold at the top, there is air in it. You can let the air out in ten minutes with a radiator key.

Written by Callum Reyes, heating engineer · Checked on 2 October 2026 · 4 minute read

You need

  • A radiator key (about £2 from a hardware shop)
  • An old cloth or a small bowl
  • For a boiler with a pressure gauge: access to the filling loop

The steps

  1. Turn the heating off and wait 20 minutes, until the radiators are cool. If the pump runs, it can pull more air into the system.
  2. Start with the radiator that is farthest from the boiler on the lowest floor. Then work towards the boiler, and go upstairs last.
  3. Find the bleed valve. It is a small square pin at the top corner of the radiator, at one end.
  4. Hold the cloth under the valve and turn the key a quarter turn to the left. You will hear the air come out.
  5. Close the valve when water comes out in a steady line and not in drops. Turn the key to the right, tight but not with force.
  6. Do the same on each radiator that has a cold top.

After you finish: check the pressure

When air leaves the system, the pressure falls. Look at the gauge on the boiler. With cold radiators it should show between 1 and 1.5 bar. If it is below 1 bar, open the filling loop slowly until the needle is at 1.2 bar, then close it fully.

Then turn the heating on again and check after an hour that each radiator is hot from the top to the bottom.

When to call an engineer

Call an engineer if you need to bleed the same radiator more than two times in one winter, if the water that comes out is black or brown, or if a radiator stays cold at the bottom. Cold at the bottom means sludge, not air, and a bleed does not help.
