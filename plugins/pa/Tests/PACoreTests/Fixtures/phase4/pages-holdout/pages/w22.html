Lesson 3 of 10 · about 8 minutes
Build a hot compost heap
A hot heap makes compost in 6 to 8 weeks. A cold heap needs a year. The difference is size, the mix of materials, and air. In this lesson you build a heap that heats to 55 °C or more in its first week.
Size comes first
A heap must be at least one cubic metre: one metre wide, one metre deep and one metre high. A smaller heap loses its heat faster than the microbes can make it.1 If you do not have enough material, collect it in bags until you do, and then build the heap in one day.
The mix
Use about two buckets of browns for each bucket of greens. Browns are dry and full of carbon: dead leaves, torn cardboard, straw, wood chips. Greens are wet and full of nitrogen: grass cuttings, kitchen peelings, fresh weeds with no seeds, coffee grounds.
Cut or tear each piece to less than 5 cm. Small pieces rot faster because the microbes work on the surface.
Build it in layers
1. Put 10 cm of thick twigs on bare soil, so that air can come in from below.
2. Add 15 cm of browns, then 8 cm of greens.
3. Water each pair of layers until it is as wet as a sponge that you pressed out.
4. Repeat until the heap is one metre high. Finish with browns.
5. Cover the top with old carpet or a sheet of cardboard to keep the heat in and the rain out.
Tip: Push a metal rod into the middle of the heap. After three days, pull it out and touch it. If it is too hot to hold, the heap works.
Do not add these
Do not add cooked food, meat, fish, dairy, cat or dog waste, or weeds with seeds or thick roots such as bindweed. Diseased plants are safe only if the heap stays above 55 °C for three days.2
In the next lesson you will learn when to turn the heap. For now, leave it for four days and measure its temperature each morning.
1. The surface of a small heap is large for its volume, so it cools fast.
2. Most plant diseases and weed seeds die at this temperature.
