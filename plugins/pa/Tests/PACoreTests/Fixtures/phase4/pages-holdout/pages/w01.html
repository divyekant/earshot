How I fixed a rattling sash window for under £30
Posted on 19 October 2026 by Greta · Filed under Windows · 14 comments

Our front bedroom window has rattled in every strong wind since we moved in. Last month I fixed it in one afternoon, and I want to write down what I did before I forget.

The rattle came from a gap between the lower sash and the staff bead. Over the years the bead had worn, and someone had painted over the gap and not repaired it. You could move the sash about four millimetres back and forward with one finger.

What I bought

I bought two lengths of new staff bead with a brush pile strip already in it (£19), a small box of 40 mm brass screws (£4) and a tube of decorators' caulk (£5). The total was £28. I already had a mitre saw, a chisel and a pull bar.

How it went

First I cut the paint line with a sharp knife, so that the old bead came off and did not take the paint of the frame with it. The old bead was nailed on. I pulled it from the middle, not from the ends, because the ends split easily.

Then I cut the new bead with a 45 degree mitre at each corner. Measure each side separately. My frame was 6 mm wider at the top than at the bottom, which is normal for a house of 1890.

I fixed the new bead with screws, not nails. With screws you can move the bead a little later, if the sash sticks when the weather turns damp. I left a gap about the thickness of a bank card between the bead and the sash, so that the brush strip touches the sash but does not hold it.

The window now slides with two fingers and does not rattle at all. The room is also warmer, which I did not expect. The whole job took three and a half hours with a break for tea.

One warning: if your house was painted before 1970, the old paint can have lead in it. Do not sand it dry. I used a wet cloth and a scraper, and I put the dust in a closed bag.
