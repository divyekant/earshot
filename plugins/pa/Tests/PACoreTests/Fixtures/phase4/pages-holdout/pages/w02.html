Rewetting of drained upland peat: water table and carbon flux after eight years

Ingrid Solheim¹, Tariq Mbeki¹, Rosalind Achterberg², Wen-Li Castaño¹

¹ Department of Environmental Science, University of Dunmarrow · ² West Fells Peat Partnership
Received 14 January 2026 · Accepted 2 June 2026 · Published 19 August 2026

Abstract

Drained peat is a large source of carbon dioxide. Many projects block drainage ditches to raise the water table again, but few have measured the result for more than three years. We report eight years of measurements from Sable Moss, a blanket bog of 310 hectares that was drained in the 1960s and rewetted in 2017 with 4,200 peat and timber dams.

The mean water table in summer rose from 38 cm below the surface before the work to 11 cm in the third year, and stayed between 9 and 13 cm from then on. The cover of Sphagnum moss increased from 6 percent to 41 percent of the surface.

The site was a net source of 2.9 tonnes of carbon dioxide equivalent per hectare and year before the work. It became a weak source in years one to four, because methane emissions increased by a factor of three. From year five the site was a net sink, with a mean of 1.2 tonnes per hectare and year in years five to eight.

We conclude that the climate benefit of rewetting comes later than most project plans assume, and that a monitoring period of three years gives a wrong picture. Dams that failed in the first two winters (7 percent of all dams) explain most of the difference between the wet and the dry parts of the site.

Keywords: blanket bog, ditch blocking, methane, net ecosystem exchange, restoration, Sphagnum

Plain language summary

When a bog is drained, the peat dries and gives off carbon dioxide. We blocked the ditches of a drained bog and measured it for eight years. The bog became wet again in three years. In the first four years it still warmed the climate a little, because wet peat gives off methane. After that it started to take carbon out of the air. Projects that stop measuring after three years will miss this change.
