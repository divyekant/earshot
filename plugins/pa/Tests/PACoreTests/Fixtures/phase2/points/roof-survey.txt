Survey report: the roof of 8 Mill Lane

Prepared for the owners after the visit of 3 October. The surveyor, Owen Pike, spent two hours on the roof and in the loft.

The main problem is water that enters at the valley between the two roof slopes at the back of the house. The lead in the valley is old and has two splits, each about ten centimetres long. In heavy rain, water runs under the slates and into the loft. We saw dark stains on the timbers below the valley, and the insulation there is wet.

The slates on the front slope are in fair condition. About a dozen are cracked or have slipped, mostly near the ridge. These can be replaced one by one with matching reclaimed slates. The ridge tiles are firm, but the mortar between them is soft and has gaps in four places.

The chimney stack is sound. The flashing around the stack is lead and is in good order. The pots are not used, and the owners may want to cap them to keep birds out.

In the loft, the timbers are dry except near the valley. There is no sign of rot or insects in the rest of the structure. The felt under the slates is the old bitumen type. It is torn in a few places but still does its job.

The gutters at the back are full of moss and leaves. The downpipe at the corner is cracked, and water runs down the wall when it rains. This explains the green mark on the back wall of the kitchen.

The valley repair is urgent and should happen before the winter, because each storm makes the damage to the timbers worse. The repair needs the slates on both sides of the valley lifted, the old lead removed, new lead laid on new boards, and the slates put back.

The other work can wait until spring. This includes the cracked slates on the front, the ridge mortar, the gutters and the downpipe. The owners can also ask for the chimney pots to be capped at the same time.

The estimate for the valley repair is 2,400 pounds, and the estimate for all the other work together is about 1,600 pounds. Both prices include scaffolding for the back of the house. If both jobs are done together in spring, the scaffolding is needed only once, which saves about 400 pounds. But the valley should not wait.

After the repair, the wet insulation in the loft should be taken out and replaced once the timbers are dry. The owners can do this themselves. A dehumidifier in the loft for two weeks will help the timbers dry.

Owen Pike, roof surveyor
