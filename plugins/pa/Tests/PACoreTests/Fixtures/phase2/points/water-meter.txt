Notes from the call with the water company about the high bill

Date of the call: Tuesday morning. I spoke with an adviser called Priya for about twenty minutes. Reference number for the call: WC-55102.

The bill for the last six months was 412 pounds. The bill for the same months last year was 186 pounds. Nothing has changed in the house. There are still two of us, and we have not filled a pool or watered a big garden.

Priya says the meter reading shows that we used more than twice as much water as last year, so the company thinks there is a leak. She asked me to do a simple test. Turn off every tap and every machine that uses water, then read the meter, wait one hour without using any water, and read it again. If the number goes up, water is escaping somewhere.

I did the test in the afternoon. The meter went up by 38 litres in one hour. That is about 900 litres a day, which matches the high bill.

Then I looked for the leak. The toilets do not run. The taps do not drip. There is no wet patch in the house. But the grass by the front path is always soft, even in dry weather. The pipe from the meter to the house runs under that path.

The leak is most likely on the supply pipe under the front path, which is our responsibility and not the responsibility of the water company. Priya explained that the company owns the pipe up to the meter at the boundary, and the owner owns the pipe from the meter to the house.

The good news is that the company has a leak allowance. If we repair the leak within 30 days and send proof, the company will refund the extra charge for the water that we lost. Proof can be an invoice from a plumber, or photos of the repair with the date. After the repair, we must send a new meter reading so they can check that the use is normal again.

The company can also offer a free repair of the supply pipe once, if the leak is within a certain distance of the meter and the pipe is easy to reach. Priya will ask the leak team and call back within five working days. If they do not offer it, we can use our own plumber.

Things to do: book a plumber to find the exact place of the leak; keep the invoice; take photos before and after; send the meter readings; and ask about the refund in writing.

I also asked whether our home insurance covers the repair. Priya did not know. I should check the policy, because some policies cover the cost of finding a leak, but not the repair of the pipe.

Next call with the company: after the repair, with the reference number above.
