Letter from the eye clinic after the appointment

Dear patient,

Thank you for coming to the eye clinic at St Anne's Hospital on 14 May. This letter tells you the results of the tests and what happens next. A copy goes to your family doctor.

You came to the clinic because your optician found a raised pressure in both eyes at your last eye test. The pressure in an eye can damage the nerve at the back of the eye over time. This condition is called glaucoma. Many people with a raised pressure never get glaucoma, so we do more tests to decide.

At the clinic we measured the pressure again, looked at the nerve with a scan, and tested your field of vision. The tests show that the pressure in both eyes is higher than normal, but there is no damage to the nerve and your field of vision is normal. This means that you do not have glaucoma at present.

Because the pressure is high, there is a risk that damage starts in the future. The risk is moderate in your case. We talked about two choices: to start eye drops now, or to watch the pressure with regular checks.

We agreed that you will start one eye drop each evening in both eyes, to lower the pressure and reduce the risk of damage. The drop is called latanoprost. Your family doctor will give you the prescription. Use one drop in each eye every evening, at about the same time. Keep the bottle in the fridge before you open it. After you open it, use it within four weeks.

Some people notice that the eyes go a little red in the first weeks. This usually gets better. Over a long time, the drop can make the eyelashes longer and darker, and in a few people it changes the colour of the iris a little. If your eyes become painful, very red or your sight changes, stop the drop and call the clinic or your doctor.

We will see you again in the clinic in four months, to check the pressure and to see whether the drop works for you. You will get the appointment by post. Please bring the bottle of drops to the appointment, and do not stop the drops before it.

You can still drive. Your sight meets the standard for driving. You do not need to tell the licensing agency, as you do not have glaucoma.

If you have questions about the drops, the pharmacist can help. For questions about this letter, call the clinic secretary on a weekday.

Yours sincerely,

Dr Hannah Fischer, consultant eye surgeon
